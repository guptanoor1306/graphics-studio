# Learned examples: script → storyboard → final graphic

Each row is one storyboard beat. **Graphic** = a designed frame exists (file named by timestamp in `references/<video>_finals/`). **Footage** = anchor only on camera, no designed frame. **Edit** = called for in the storyboard but no frame was designed; it was left to the editor (template text or a reused animation).

Storyboard shorthand: **OSA** = on-screen animation over the anchor shot (anchor stays visible, graphic sits in the empty side of frame). **OST** = on-screen text (title or heading over the anchor). **FSS** = full-screen slide (anchor off screen, graphic fills the frame). **Badger** = a cut-in clip from another video. **"Continue / highlight as per VO"** = the same frame stays up while highlights move with the voice-over; no new frame is designed.

## Patterns that held across all videos

1. **Every section heading gets a title frame** (OST). The keyword is set big and bold; the words around it are smaller.
2. **Every number the VO says gets put on screen**: a table, chart, value pill or labelled arrow. The result or answer row is always visually distinct (solid white row, amber bar, or a bold label).
3. **A document the script mentions becomes a realistic mock document** built from the script's own numbers, highlighted line by line (magnifier, blur plus callout, clipped note).
4. **A list of items gets one element per item, revealed in VO order**: cards, tiles, wedges or numbered rows. Never a static bullet list over the anchor.
5. **Growth or change over time becomes a chart, slider or growing objects**, never prose.
6. **One frame serves several beats.** "Continue / highlight as per VO" means build one master frame plus hidden states, not new frames.
7. **Footage only** for: hooks spoken to camera, rhetorical bridges ("Now let's pause here"), recaps of material already shown, caveats and risks with no new number, the conclusion and CTA, and Badger cut-ins.
8. **Definition boxes and "points to remember" lists** were left to the editor in Video 1 but designed (FSS) in Video 2. Treat them as graphics.
9. **Reference images in the storyboard are for content, not style.** Notes like "keep it in Varsity theme", "just a ref, do not keep it same" and "keep all forms in the same theme" mean: take the information and redraw it with house components.
10. **Screen captures, web pages, PDFs and tool CTAs are footage** (screen recordings), not graphics. A news article is the exception: it gets re-typeset as a panel over the anchor.
11. **Recurring devices.** A chapter-marker graphic for numbered lists ("Mistake 1…9") is re-shown before each item. A persona (Rahul, Anjali, Rohan) is the same stick-figure at a desk, renamed with a pill.
12. **Follow the script when it conflicts with the storyboard** (ITR-1 vs ITR-4), and spell-check titles. The final "ITR Filling" is a typo that shipped.
13. **Big calculation tables stay complete** (every row the VO reads) but get one PNG each. Name them `TS-Table N`. The person or entity the table belongs to stands beside it with a name pill.
14. **Totals and sums said aloud over the anchor become a pop-up window card** (OST) rather than a full-screen frame.
15. **Several storyboard tables that compare scenarios can merge into one matrix** with icons and coloured verdict pills (Video 4, gifts).
16. **A recurring keyword (HUF, ESOPs) is the hero of every section title**: giant coral or white bold, with small connecting words above and below.
17. **List of pointers → "blurred list" device**: all pointers visible but blurred on paper in a folder, each unblurred as the VO reaches it, and the frame re-shown before each pointer's section.
18. **The FSS accent follows the topic or video**: blue sky (Retirement, ITR, HUF) or warm cream with amber (Overdraft). Within one video it never changes.
19. **The topic gets a recurring prop** placed in a bottom corner of most frames: house (home loan), TAX doc + calculator + coins (tax videos), jar of cash (retirement), coins (ESOP).
20. **Text is trimmed hard.** Storyboard notes like "keep it minimal", "remove all text, just keep elements" and "do not keep all the text" are common. Show the number and the label, not the sentence.
