# Zero1 Hindi — "The Reality of Noida's Real Estate" (v2) — gold storyboard corpus

**Episode shape.** A four-chapter explainer on whether Noida property is worth it. The protagonist is **Arjun**, the wooden-block salaried everyman in a red tee with a pouch pocket. He appears from Chapter 2 onwards and carries every reaction beat: head-scratch, "?", thumbs-up. The world mixes two looks. Arjun, crowds, construction sites, offices and land plots are **handcrafted toy dioramas** on a crumpled-paper sky. The skyline and township sets are **photo collages of real Noida landmarks** (Supernova, World Trade Tower, Mall of India, the metro, the Buddha statue). Those collage sets are reused as master plates (`skyline.jpg` for the intro and Chapter 2, `BUILDINGS.jpg` for Chapter 1, plus the society-avenue set and the gated-society set). The recurring devices are:
- a **chapter reveal** card (italic "Chapter-N" plus a bold condensed title over the master set)
- **price-tag banners** hung on strings over buildings
- **tables hung on ropes** (upfront costs, EMI math, salary needed)
- **greyscale-the-world, keep-one-thing-in-colour** for focus
- a **newspaper/headline** source device
- a **phone UI** close-up (maintenance in #46, "Registered" in #62)
- a **see-saw** that turns into "STRESS TEST"
- a **checklist clipboard**

The story runs from hype (the skyline, +125%, the demand curve) to paradox (52% of demand vs 23% of supply, survivorship bias), then affordability (₹2 Cr → ₹3.79 Cr, ₹72 L CTC), then quality (decay, water, maintenance) and title (OC vs registry). It closes on a due-diligence checklist with Arjun's thumbs-up.

## Designer deviations & additions

- **SOP mismatch (#8).** The final VO has **3** questions, but the PSD uses the **old 4-item** SOP. ## Top 10 storyboarding moves in this video

1. "we'll figure 3 things" → the SOP "?" is assembled from shards of the previous crowd and under-construction frame → the hook's image literally becomes the episode's question (MORPH).
2. "1.18 LAKH CRORE" → the number is written out with zeros running off the frame over the locked-gate set → the viewer feels the scale instead of reading it (NUMOBJ, TYPO, CALLBACK).
3. "Hume sirf wohi flats dikhte hai jo value mein double ho gaye" → the zeros turn into binoculars framing tagged premium villas → survivorship bias becomes a narrow field of view (MORPH, LIT).
4. "Iska man set hai iss 2 crore ke flat par" → the whole avenue goes greyscale except one tower, and a yellow "₹2 Crore" sign is planted beside Arjun → desire is shown through colour (GRADE, NUMOBJ).
5. "padega ₹3.79 crore ka" → the same greyscale set, with the ₹2 Crore sign crossed out and "Actual Value ₹3 Crore" overhead → the callback makes the gap visible on the same object (CALLBACK, BUILD).
6. "EMI uski salary ki 30% hai" → the formula is printed on a cloth banner hung over a tower → the maths stays physically inside the property world (NUMOBJ, LIT).
7. "99.5% log afford hi nahi kar sakte" → Arjun with a "✓ ₹71,00,000 CTC" card stays in colour inside a sea of greyscale office workers → "exception" is shown, not stated (GRADE, EMO).
8. "groundwater … zyada extract ho raha hai" → the camera drops from the #48 tower site into a soil cross-section with pile foundations → a CAM move turns an invisible resource into a place (CAM, CALLBACK).
9. "prices premium hain, lekin premium ghar certain nahi" → a see-saw weighted with cash vs a weak property, then balanced, then cracking into "STRESS TEST" → one object carries three VO turns and hands off to the next section (DEVICE, HERO).
10. "flat abhi bhi Arjun ke naam par nahi hai" → a security guard stops Arjun at the boom barrier, and the same frame becomes the Chapter-4 card → the abstract legal point becomes a physical denial of entry (LIT, EMO, CALLBACK).
