# How the Zero1 Hindi team storyboards (and how you must)

Learned from the team's own storyboard docs for V1, V2 and V3: every VO line and the cue they wrote under it, compared with the frame the designer finally made (`gold/v1_corpus.md`, `gold/v2_corpus.md`, `gold/v3_corpus.md`).

## The one idea that matters
**The team does not design slides. They direct a camera through one miniature toy world, and every VO line becomes something that physically happens in that world.**

Their cues read like a film director's notes:
- *"Lekin se camera pan down hota hai: pehle crowd, phir society ka darwaza, end mein under-construction society."*
- *"Jo 41 mein map ki outline hai woh niche wali bank ki outline ban jayegi."*
- *"Upar ke 18 lakh ke 0 ko hi binoculars mein convert kardena."*
- *"Ek ek karke notice ake chipak jayenge ghar ke bahar."*
- *"30 wale graphic se loan attachments hata do, ghar ko 83% tak red karke niche likh do Borrowers."*

A medium storyboard has the opposite habits:
- a different picture for every line
- text on an empty sky backdrop
- numbers shown as big type with nothing around them
- vague cues like "show gold investment graphic"

**Your job is to write the team's kind.**

## The procedure (do every pass, in order)

### Pass 0: Read and inventory (no ideas yet)
Read the whole script twice. Write down, briefly:
- **Episode question.** The one money question the video answers.
- **Shape.** Comparison (several Arjuns or cases), Investigation (one decision, chapters = questions) or Life-story (one person + one object across time). See `storyboard_rules.md` §1.
- **Arjun.** Which variant (city / Kerala / Andhra / Meghalaya / new state). His situation at the start and at the end (his arc).
- **Every number** and **every source** the script states, in order.
- **Every list** (options, steps, costs, reasons).
- **Turning points**: "Lekin / But / Magar / Actually / Right?" lines. Each becomes a pivot beat.

### Pass 1: Build the world bible (this is what makes it one film)
Before any beat, decide:

1. **The world and its sets.** List 4–8 physical **sets** the whole episode will live in, each regionally authentic, with what the camera can see there. Examples from the gold videos:
   - Arjun's kirana / home / township plaza / builder's office / jeweller counter
   - the India map
   - the timeline strip
   - the newspaper
   - an abstract paper "lab" for dot/ball data

   Write a regional-research note for each, e.g. *"Andhra: tiled-roof houses, kolam, aunties in sarees, bank boards in Telugu-English"*. The team always asks for this ("is chapter ki sari chezein andhra style… do your research").

2. **Two or three recurring devices** you introduce early and pay off later. **Use the house devices first** (below). Invent a new device only when none fits, and then put it *inside* a house device rather than replacing one. The team and the audience recognise these, and they reuse finished design. Gold examples:
   - the notebook **sticker tag** (V1 intro → V1 chapter 1 → conclusion)
   - the **timeline spine** (V1, V3)
   - the **newspaper with flipping pages** (V2)
   - **option boards** (V3)
   - the **checklist** (V2)
   - the **see-saw** (V2)
   - the **India map** (V1)
   - the **thought bubble** (all three)

   **House devices** (channel history: reuse whenever the content matches):
   | Content | House device |
   |---|---|
   | Arjun's facts / who he is | notebook **sticker tag** hanging on twine (NAME / AGE / SALARY / INFO / STATE) |
   | Where / which state | **wooden India map** with Arjun figurine, state lifts out for the chapter reveal |
   | History, years, "X saal baad" | **timeline spine** (vertical line, red dot, Impact year, grid patch, cut-out scene per stop). **Future projections return to the same spine** ("5 Years Later", "10 Years Later") |
   | Several sources / headlines | **newspaper** whose pages flip per headline |
   | Options / chapters of a comparison | **rope-hung option boards** that come down one by one ("Option-1 / GOLD COIN OR BAR"), reused as the closing boards |
   | Multi-step money maths, tax rules | stage the steps literally, then **end on one desk-top ledger** (grid paper on the craft desk: header, rows, NET/FINAL bar). Tax rules = a comparison table on the desk |
   | Costs that add up / EMI maths | **hung table** revealing row by row, worried Arjun beside |
   | Results of several options at 2 points in time | **one hub/ring layout** (centre value + instruments around) updated per period, not one beat per number |
   | Checks to do | **clipboard checklist** with a changing photo |
   | Trade-off / risk | **see-saw** |
   | Paradox / the episode's questions | **"?" shatter** + **numbered polaroids that name the chapters** |
   | Imagination, memory, doubt | **thought bubble**, "?" over Arjun's head |
   | Big reveal | scene darkened + **gold number** |
   | "Only a few / exception" | **greyscale crowd**, Arjun in colour |

3. **One hero object** if the story has one (V3: the necklace; V2: the ₹2 Crore flat; V1: the house that turns red). It appears in the intro, carries the middle, and returns in the conclusion.

4. **One or two hero animations**: elaborate multi-step physical sequences that are the episode's showpiece. Gold examples:
   - red balls merge, roll and push the blue ball out of frame (V1 #76–77)
   - the same Khasi house from 1920 to now while families change around it (V1 #64)
   - the baby grows while gold fills the chest beside her (V3 #4)
   - binoculars that scan the towers and land on SURVIVORSHIP BIAS (V2 #19–20)

### Pass 2: Split into beats
- **One beat per VO sentence, often per clause.** Split at every number, every list item, every "Lekin / But", every new idea. About 6–9 beats per minute; V1 had ~86 beats in ~11 min.
- Build states (`_b`, `_c`) count as beats for rhythm, but **number your cues like the team**: one `#N` per VO sentence or tight group, builds as `_b`/`_c` states inside it.
- **Budget:** cue count ≈ the team's ~6 per minute (about 1 cue per 25 spoken words), never more than 1.2× the script's sentence count. **Distinct new gen plates ≤ ~25 per episode.** Reuse sets and plates; a storyboard that needs 50 new paintings won't get made.

### Pass 3: Draft each beat with the move library
For every beat ask, in this order, and take the **first** answer that works:

1. **Can I literalise it?** (LIT) If this line happened in Arjun's toy world, what would the camera SEE? Turn the verb into an action and the abstraction into an object. See the literalisation bank below.
2. **Can the previous frame transform into it?** (MORPH / BUILD) A shape, object or number from the last frame becomes this one, or the same set changes one thing.
3. **Can the camera get there?** (CAM / MATCH) Zoom out to the map, pan down to the crowd, tilt up to the sky, dive underground, push into a bubble, top cam on a table, POV, match cut to another place or time.
4. **Is it a number?** (NUMOBJ / DATA) Stage it on or with an object:
   - a price tag on the building
   - % fill of the house
   - dots that fill
   - the timeline stopping at the year with the figure beside it
   - a calculation typo above Arjun scratching his head
   - table rows revealing
   - a number running off the frame with extra zeros
5. **Is it Arjun's thought, memory, doubt or imagination?** (BUBBLE / EMO) A thought bubble, low-opacity "imagined" objects, "?" over his head, his face reacting.
6. **Is it a source?** (SRC) Inside the world: a newspaper headline, an article on the office wall, a paper with a rubber stamp, a sheet held by a hand. Not a standalone slide.
7. **Only then, a typo** (TYPO). Text-led beats are for pivot words ("LEKIN!"), questions ("SALARY ?") and the takeaway line. Even then, put the typo **over the current shot** ("show typo above previous frame"), not on a blank backdrop.

**Rules the blind test taught:**
- **The episode's key gain/loss number lands on people**: Arjun's or his family's faces/reaction under or beside the number, not only on an object (V3 #49.2: the sad family under "30.6%").
- **Emotional climax = EMO close-up** (tearful face, the hero object shining), never a gag or a cutaway.
- **Market-price claims keep the real chart** as a SRC beat with a home in the world (the camera tilts up from the shop into a chart in the sky), even if objects also carry the number.
- **Long calculations:** literal step-by-step, then one readable summary (desk ledger). Don't spread the summary over 8 beats.
- **Real institutions by name** on signage when the script implies them (Muthoot, Manappuram and a bank for "NBFCs and banks" in Kerala; SBI; NSE; SEBI stamp) as kit text, never as generated logos.
- **Two places, one movement** (migration, trade): a two-place diorama with people walking between, rather than a map with arrows.
- **Finance copy must be exact.** Fund costs = TER (expense ratio), exit load, tracking difference. Never add items the VO doesn't state; mark guesses `VERIFY`.

Write each cue in the team's voice (Hinglish + English, as in `gold/`). It must have:
- **what the camera sees**
- **what changes or moves**
- **the exact on-screen text**
- **how we arrive from the previous beat**

### Pass 4: The continuity chain
Read the beats as a film. For every cut, write the transition: CAM, MATCH, MORPH or BUILD. A hard cut is allowed only at a chapter start or a deliberate jolt ("hard transition to the wedding").
- **Every new set needs an entry move.**
- **Every chapter needs at least one MORPH.**
- **Every device introduced is paid off later (CALLBACK).**
- **Chapter reveals come out of the world**:
  - zoom out to the map → the state lifts out + "Chapter-N" title
  - go back to the SOP and zoom into question 2
  - the camera rises over the skyline and the title appears in the sky

  Never a title card on blank paper.

### Pass 5: Self-critique and rewrite (mandatory)
Score your draft with the 9-point rubric below. Then **rewrite at least the weakest 10% of beats**: every TYPO beat that could be LIT, every number floating without an object, every new set without an entry move. Score again. Do not deliver below an average of 4.

| # | Criterion | 5 = | 1 = |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Literalness | ≥ 70% of beats show the idea as an action/object | mostly text on backdrop |
| 2 | Continuity | every cut is in-world (CAM/MATCH/MORPH/BUILD); persistent sets carry 3–8 beats each | new picture every line |
| 3 | Arjun | in or implied in most beats; does, reacts, thinks; has an arc | absent or decorative |
| 4 | Numbers | every number staged on an object, revealed by animation | numbers as floating type |
| 5 | Devices & callbacks | 2–3 devices introduced and paid off; intro ↔ conclusion callback | none |
| 6 | Variety & rhythm | no 3 consecutive beats of the same move; TYPO-only ≤ 15%; ≥ 1 hero animation | monotone |
| 7 | Region & specificity | sets, people, objects regionally authentic and specific | generic stock scenes |
| 8 | Fidelity | every script number, name and list item present and correct; SOP = chapters; nothing invented | missing or wrong facts |
| 9 | House devices, budget & producibility | house devices reused where they fit; cues ≈ 6/min; new gen plates ≤ ~25; every camera move = 2 keyframes; ≤ 2 hero animations | new device every chapter, 1.5×+ beats, choreography everywhere |

Print the scores (with one line of evidence each) at the end of the storyboard.

### Pass 6: Production plan
Turn the storyboard into frames:
- **A camera move = keyframes.** Give a start frame and an end frame (e.g. `0.12` wide on the house, `0.12_b` the Kochi map); the editor animates between them.
- **A build = states** (`_b`, `_c`).
- **For each frame, say how it gets made:**
  - an **existing plate** from `assets/plates/` (real textless scenes from past episodes; see `plates_index.md`), or
  - a **kit asset**, or
  - a **gen** plate (new image prompt), plus
  - the kit's text, tags, tables and other layers on top.

  Reuse plates when the set matches. Generate only what is new.

## Signature patterns (the team's best moves; aim for each at least once per episode)
1. **One object, three turns.** A single device carries three consecutive VO turns: the see-saw goes cash-heavy → balanced → cracks into "STRESS TEST"; the house gets loans → fills 83% red → splits into 4 → notices stick on it.
2. **Number morph.** A figure on screen becomes the next device: the zeros of ₹1,18,000… become binoculars; the year line rises into the demand curve; the map outline becomes the bank outline.
3. **Greyscale for desire or exception.** The world goes grey; only the wanted object (the ₹2 Crore tower) or the exception (Arjun among the 99.5%) stays in colour.
4. **Physical denial.** A legal or financial "no" becomes something physically stopping Arjun: the guard at the boom barrier, the phone's "Register" button failing, a locked gate in front of the crowd.
5. **Imagined = low opacity, past = sepia, memory = thought bubble.**
6. **The callback close.** The conclusion reuses the intro's set and device with the new understanding added (the sticker tags return with the three Arjuns; the necklace from Ch-2 at the wedding).

## Producibility (the designers build in Photoshop and animate in After Effects)
- Each camera move must be doable as **two keyframes of the same plate** (zoom or pan inside one large image) or **a crossfade/morph between two plates**. Write both keyframes in `Frames:`.
- Character animation stays simple: a pose change between keyframes (walks in, scratches head, smiles), never choreography. Elaborate multi-step physical animation is reserved for the 1–2 **hero animations**.
- A chain of three or more camera moves without a hold is too much: hold the set for 2–4 beats of builds between moves.
- Reuse beats producibility: the same plate with a new text, tag, tint or state is the cheapest good frame.

## When the user gives a storyboard (SB-given mode)
The team's cues are the **intent**. Keep them, then raise the quality:
1. **Keep every camera move, morph and callback** the cue asks for. Never flatten "camera zooms out to the map" into a static title card. Split it into keyframes.
2. **Fill the gaps** with the method. Gaps look like "Reference below", "TOP CAM", "#49 Graphic –", "FIND CONNECTOR FOR CHAPTER REVEAL", or VO with no cue. Write the full cue in their voice and mark it `[added]`.
3. **Upgrade weak cues.** A cue that is only "TYPO X" over nothing becomes typo-over-the-current-shot. A number without an object gets one. Mark these `[upgraded]` with the original kept.
4. **Check the cue against the VO.** Flag numbers, names or SOP items that differ.
5. Then run Pass 5 and Pass 6 as usual.

## Anti-patterns (each one makes output "medium")
- A different backdrop every beat.
- Sky-paper + text as the default frame.
- Numbers as floating type.
- A list as a bullet slide.
- Sources as screenshots on an empty page.
- Generic stock scenes with no Arjun.
- Chapter titles on blank paper.
- Cues without camera/motion.
- Forgetting the intro device by the end.
- Two typo-only beats in a row.
